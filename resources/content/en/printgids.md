# Print guide

For each part, this page says what to order from a print shop or how to print it at home. The same text is included as a PDF in every set.

Everything except the board can be skipped. Whatever you skip, you take from your own box: the cards, money and pawns of the game you already own stay in use. Only the board is required, because it is the part that shows your streets. On the print page you untick every part you do not want to print.

You can mix parts from the print-shop set and the home-print set. For example, have the board printed at a shop and print the money at home.

Making more than one set, say one for every group in a class? Choose the number of sets at the bottom of the print page. The bar counts the sheets for all sets together, and every instruction says how much you print or order in total.

## Start with the proof sheet

Print the proof sheet on A4 at actual size, never on "fit to page". The sheet has a 100 mm ruler, the outline of a bank card (85.60 × 53.98 mm) and a test print of a corner space and a regular space, with their sizes.

Lay a bank card on the outline. If it fits exactly and the ruler measures 100 mm, your printer is scaling correctly. Then lay the corner space on your board. If it fits, you can order or print the rest. If it does not, enter the measurements of your board in the settings of your edition.

The proof sheet is not part of the print-shop order. You print that one yourself, at home or at a copy shop.

## Printing at home: what you need

Printing at home saves the print shop, but it takes time. Plan on an evening: roughly an hour or two for the board (cutting out more than 40 spaces) and another hour and a half for the cards (cutting). The play money is quicker.

You need:

- A sharp craft knife or sharp scissors. A knife gives cleaner straight edges.
- A metal ruler. A plastic one gets nicked quickly.
- A cutting mat, or a piece of thick cardboard under the paper.
- Sticker paper for your printer: A4, matte, for inkjet or laser. The packaging says which.
- Paper of 160 to 200 gsm for the cards. Check your printer manual first, because many home printers stop at about 160 gsm. If yours cannot take it, print on plain paper and glue the card to cardboard.
- A printer that prints on both sides, for the cards. Without one it works too: you turn the stack over yourself.
- A bank card, to press the stickers down and to check the proof sheet.

Always print at actual size. Each program words it a little differently:

- **Browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox):** set "Scale" to "Default" or 100%. Turn off "Fit to page" and choose "None" for margins.
- **Adobe Reader:** under "Page Sizing & Handling" choose "Actual size". If it says "Fit" or "Shrink oversized pages", the print will be scaled.
- **Preview on a Mac:** untick "Scale to fit" and set "Scale" to 100%.

Check it with the proof sheet: the ruler on it must measure exactly 100 mm.

Checklist before you start:

- The proof sheet is printed and the ruler measures 100 mm.
- The bank card fits exactly on the outline.
- The sticker paper and card paper suit your printer.
- You have a sharp knife, a metal ruler and a cutting mat.
- The alignment test for the cards is printed and the crosses sit on top of each other.
- You have a few hours to spare.

## Board

A modern board is square, about 50 × 50 cm. The size differs a few millimetres between releases, so the site works from the board profile of your edition. The board is a sticker for the board you already own.

### At the print shop

Order as custom-size vinyl sticker, white, matte laminated, with repositionable adhesive. The panels are named A to D. They stop 1 mm before each fold line, because a sticker across a fold tears or creases. Upload each PDF as it is. The size comes from the board profile of your edition: the print page and the instruction name the size of your board. For the standard board it is:

`4 pieces · each 248 × 248 mm · 3 mm bleed and crop marks`

Every PDF with bleed has crop marks outside the bleed, and a TrimBox and BleedBox, so the print shop knows where to cut.

### At home

Buy full A4 sticker sheets, matte, for your type of printer (inkjet or laser). Each space is a separate sticker and the centre comes in four pieces. Print at actual size and cut with a knife along a ruler.

`7 A4 sheets for the standard board · spaces 0.5 mm smaller than on the board`

### Sticking it on

1. Degrease the board and fit each panel dry first.
2. Lay panel A against the outer corner of the board and pull off 5 cm of backing paper.
3. Press down with a bank card, from the middle outward, and peel off the rest of the backing slowly. Do the same with panels B, C and D.
4. Fold the board and check that the fold is free. If a sticker touches the fold, peel it off and place it again.

For the home version, start at Go and work along the edge. If an air bubble is trapped, prick it with a pin and smooth it out.

### Without sticker paper or without a board

No sticker paper, or no old board? Print the same sheets on plain paper of 90 to 120 gsm and glue the spaces onto sturdy cardboard, such as a moving box or greyboard. Cut the cardboard to the size of the board (50 × 50 cm for the standard board), cut out the spaces and glue them in place with a glue stick or spray glue, starting with Go in a corner. Let it dry for an hour under a few books. Want to fold the board? Cut the cardboard through the middle and tape the halves together on the back with wide tape.

`the same A4 sheets · plain paper · cardboard the size of the board`

## Cards and deeds

The cards of your old game can go. You print the Chance cards, the Community Chest cards and the title deeds new, front and back. That is 16 Chance cards, 16 Community Chest cards and 28 title deeds.

### At the print shop

Order as a card set with a custom design per card, 350 gsm matte, rounded corners. Upload the PDF as it is: each card is on its own page, front and back alternating, with 3 mm bleed and crop marks.

`60 cards (61 with the house rules card) · 63 × 88 mm · each with its own front and back`

### At home

Print the cards on both sides of 160 to 200 gsm matte paper, flipping on the long edge. Each sheet has 9 fronts, and the page after it has the backs mirrored, so every back lands exactly behind its front. The backs run on 1.5 mm past the edge, so a small shift of the printer does not show.

Print the alignment test first: the first two pages of the PDF. Hold the sheet up to the light. The crosses on the front and back should sit on top of each other, and the small scales tell you how many millimetres they are off. If the back is upside down, choose the long edge instead of the short edge in the print dialog. Then cut the cards with a knife along a ruler, on the crop marks in the margin.

`16 pages · alignment test plus 7 A4 portrait sheets, both sides · 9 cards per sheet`

## Play money

The play money is its own design with the word "play money" on it, and it does not look like real banknotes. You choose how much money to print, in the editor under Parts or on the print page under Play money:

- For 2 to 4 players: 144 notes. Every home set starts like this.
- For 5 to 8 players: 258 notes, enough for the starting money of 8 players.
- The full bank: 306 notes, at least 30 of every denomination. Every print-shop set starts like this.

### At the copy shop

Have the money printed on 90 to 120 gsm paper and cut on the crop marks. Choose SRA3.

`11, 19 or 22 sheets SRA3`

### At home

Print on plain A4 paper of 90 to 120 gsm and cut on the crop marks.

`24, 43 or 51 sheets A4`

## Pawns

Pawns are fold-up stands with a tab foot. Fold along the lines and push the foot into the slot.

### At the print shop

Print them on 350 gsm.

`1 sheet A4`

### At home

At home, 200 gsm works too.

`1 sheet A4`

## Box lid

The box lid is a sticker at the size from your board profile.

### At the print shop

Order it as custom-size vinyl sticker, matte. The PDF has 3 mm bleed and crop marks.

`1 piece · about 267 × 267 mm`

### At home

Print it on a sticker sheet. If the lid does not fit on A4, use A3.

`about 267 × 267 mm`

## If something goes wrong

A white edge after cutting usually means the printer scaled the page. Print the proof sheet again and choose "actual size". If a photo looks grainy, check the Review list in the editor: the site warns below 150 dpi, and around 300 dpi is sharp. Dull colours usually come from the paper or the print shop. Ask for a test print of one corner space if you want to be sure.
