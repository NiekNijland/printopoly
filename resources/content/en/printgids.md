# Print guide

For each part, this page says what to order from a print shop or how to print it at home. Your download has the same instructions, plus an order sheet with the exact sizes.

Only the board has to be in it. You can skip the rest and take it from your own box. On the print page you tick what goes in your set.

Printing yourself or going to a copy shop? Choose the home-print set: everything is on A4 and you do the cutting. Going to a print shop or a sticker printer? Choose the print-shop set: every part has its own size and the shop does the cutting.

Making more than one set, say one for every group in a class? Choose the number of sets at the bottom of the print page. The amounts in the instructions then cover all sets together. Cards, title deeds and notes get a small set mark (SET 1, SET 2 and so on), so the sets do not get mixed up. The PDFs of the cards and the play money already hold every set, so you print them once.

## Printing at home: what you need

Plan on an evening: about two hours for the board (cutting out more than 40 spaces) and an hour and a half for the cards. The play money is quicker.

You need:

- A sharp craft knife or sharp scissors. A knife gives cleaner straight edges.
- A metal ruler. A plastic one gets nicked quickly.
- A cutting mat, or a piece of thick cardboard under the paper.
- Sticker paper for your printer: A4, matte, for inkjet or laser. The packaging says which.
- Paper of 160 to 200 gsm for the cards. Check your printer manual first, because many home printers stop at about 160 gsm. If yours cannot take it, print on plain paper and glue the card to cardboard.
- A printer that prints on both sides, for the cards. Without one it works too: you turn the stack over yourself.
- A bank card, to press the stickers down and to check the test sheet.

Always print at actual size. Each program words it a little differently:

- **Browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox):** set "Scale" to "Default" or 100%. Turn off "Fit to page" and choose "None" for margins.
- **Adobe Reader:** under "Page Sizing & Handling" choose "Actual size". If it says "Fit" or "Shrink oversized pages", the print will be scaled.
- **Preview on a Mac:** untick "Scale to fit" and set "Scale" to 100%.

Check it with the test sheet: the ruler on it must measure exactly 100 mm.

## If something goes wrong

A white edge after cutting usually means the printer scaled the page. Print the test sheet again and choose "actual size". If a photo looks grainy, look at Check before you print at the top of the print page: it says which photo is too small for its place on paper. Choose a larger photo then, or zoom in less. Dull colours usually come from the paper or the print shop. Ask for a test print of one corner space if you want to be sure.
